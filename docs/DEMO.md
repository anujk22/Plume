# Four-minute demonstration

**0:00–0:25 — The problem.** “A methane number is easy to find. Knowing what it proves is harder. Plume lets researchers, journalists, and curious people take the evidence apart—and take a verifiable copy away.” Open the illustrated landing and the Yemen investigation.

**0:25–1:05 — Time.** Compare the April and October acquisitions. Point to the actual timestamps, shared enhancement scale, and gaps. “Two dates establish two observations. They do not prove a continuous leak or an emissions trend.”

**1:05–1:40 — What counts.** Reveal the seven product/version records for the April observation. “Seven catalog records can be one measurement. Here is exactly which evidence is shared.” Do not claim a verified cross-provider join.

**1:40–2:20 — Findings that can be inspected.** Open Newby Island. Click its established finding. Deselect September: three dates become two. Deselect August 7: recurrence becomes a single-date statement. Restore the selection.

**2:20–3:10 — The number we decline to invent.** Open the comparison assessment: real EPA annual methane mass beside real satellite acquisition context. Show quantity, time and boundary failures. Explain that the site also contains separately permitted landfill and composting processes; location alone does not resolve the emitting process.

**3:10–3:45 — Reusable evidence.** Generate the PDF and download the ZIP. Show the original record links, structured CSV/JSON/GeoJSON, source terms and checksum manifest. Notes are optional and stay separate from verified findings.

**3:45–4:00 — Why it matters.** “The outcome is an understandable case someone can review, reproduce, and follow up—not a claim that this app has repaired a leak.”

Learning discussion must describe work actually performed: CRS/mask handling, quantity semantics, revision lineage, browser workers, deterministic claims and export verification. Do not invent an experimentally disproved detection method or user adoption story.
